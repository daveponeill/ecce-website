What we study

Research

Our projects sit at the intersection of identity, culture, and practice — from how individuals come to see themselves as engineers and researchers, to the institutional conditions that shape mentorship, assessment, and wellbeing.

NSF Funded · Award #2306239 · $199,982 · 2023–2026

Engineering & Science Identity in Graduate Mentorship

How do engineering faculty understand their own identities as engineers and scientists — and how do those self-perceptions shape the way they mentor graduate students?

This project focuses on BME and MechE faculty at Northwestern, examining the relationship between faculty members' professional identities and the mentoring relationships they build with their graduate students.

Pending NSF Funding

Doctoral Wellbeing & Identity at the Qualifying Exam

Qualifying examinations are among the most consequential and stressful milestones in doctoral education. This project uses longitudinal qualitative methods and the PERMA-V(N) wellbeing framework to understand how BME doctoral students experience qualifying exams.

We are particularly interested in how wellbeing and engineering identity interact across this high-stakes transition, and what that interaction can tell us about supporting doctoral students more effectively.

Internal Funding · URAP

Testing Aids in Engineering Assessment

What do students put on authorized reference sheets, and what does that reveal about how they understand course material?

This project develops robust methods for evaluating the quality and strategic use of testing aids in undergraduate engineering courses — treating cheat sheets and reference sheets as a window into how students organize and prioritize knowledge.

Internal Funding · URAP, Murphy Society

Office Hours: Barriers, Motivations & Inclusive Practice

Office hours are an underutilized resource. This project investigates why students do and don't attend, and what instructional practices make office hours more equitable and effective.

By understanding the barriers students face and the motivations that bring them through the door, we aim to identify practices that make office hours a genuinely useful part of the learning experience for all students.
